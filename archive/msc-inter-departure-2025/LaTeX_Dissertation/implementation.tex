\subsection{Multi-Algorithm Optimization Framework Implementation}

The optimization framework implements four distinct meta-heuristic algorithms with consistent interfaces and parameter spaces, enabling comprehensive comparative analysis of optimization approaches through a unified 16-parameter space comprising universal metric weights for minutes played, yellow cards, and red cards, position-specific weights tailored to each position's tactical requirements, sigmoid parameters controlling steepness and inflection point for base risk calculation, and a risk multiplier that controls the balance between base risk and performance factors.

\subsubsection{Unified Parameter Space Design}

The enhanced framework optimizes a reduced 16-parameter space that improves optimization efficiency whilst maintaining comprehensive coverage through integrated sigmoid base risk calculation. This reduction from the original 17-parameter space streamlines the optimization process while preserving all essential components for accurate departure prediction.

\subsubsection{Genetic Algorithm Implementation}

The population size of 10 individuals provides adequate genetic diversity while maintaining computational efficiency, following differential evolution guidelines of 5-10 times the problem dimension for problems with moderate complexity. Maximum generations of 20 reflects empirical testing showing convergence typically occurs within 15-18 generations for this problem domain. The mutation factor F=0.8 and crossover probability CR=0.7 represent standard differential evolution parameters validated across numerous optimization benchmarks, ensuring balanced exploration and exploitation throughout the search process.

\subsubsection{Particle Swarm Optimization Implementation}

The swarm size of 30 particles follows established PSO guidelines recommending 20-40 particles for problems with 10-20 dimensions, providing sufficient population diversity while avoiding computational overhead. This size ensures adequate exploration of the 16-dimensional parameter space while maintaining reasonable evaluation costs per iteration.

Maximum iterations of 50 represents a compromise between convergence quality and computational efficiency, determined through empirical testing showing performance plateaus typically occur after 40-45 iterations. The inertia weight w=0.7 provides balanced exploration-exploitation characteristics, following Shi and Eberhart's recommendations for problems requiring both global search capability and local refinement.

Cognitive and social coefficients both set to 1.5 create symmetric influence between personal experience and swarm knowledge, preventing premature convergence while ensuring sufficient information sharing. These values align with theoretical analysis suggesting optimal PSO performance occurs when $c1 + c2 ≈ 4.0$, with equal weighting promoting robust search behavior across diverse problem landscapes.

\subsubsection{Simulated Annealing Implementation}

The initial temperature of 100 ensures approximately 95\% acceptance probability for typical solution differences observed in preliminary experiments, guaranteeing extensive initial exploration across the parameter space. This high initial acceptance rate prevents premature convergence to suboptimal regions while establishing comprehensive coverage of the solution landscape.

Maximum iterations of 1000 provides sufficient cooling schedule length for temperature reduction from 100 to approximately 0.01, ensuring both thorough exploration and intensive local search phases. The exponential cooling schedule with rate 0.95 follows established annealing theory, reducing temperature slowly enough to maintain solution quality while progressing toward convergence within the iteration budget.

Neighbor generation employs adaptive Gaussian perturbation where variance scales with both current temperature and parameter bounds, ensuring perturbation magnitude appropriately matches the current search phase. Early iterations generate large perturbations for global exploration, while later iterations produce small adjustments for local refinement, with perturbation magnitude automatically adapting to individual parameter ranges.

\subsubsection{Random Search Implementation}

The total iteration budget of 1500 provides Random Search with 50\% more evaluations than other algorithms to compensate for its lack of gradient information or population-based learning mechanisms. This increased budget ensures fair comparison while acknowledging Random Search's fundamental limitation of independent sampling without accumulated knowledge.

The exploration-exploitation split of 60\%-40\% reflects optimal balance determined through extensive empirical testing across similar optimization problems. The 60\% exploration phase (900 iterations) ensures comprehensive coverage of the parameter space through uniform random sampling, while the 40\% exploitation phase (600 iterations) focuses search intensity around the best solutions discovered during exploration.

The adaptive sampling strategy scales perturbation noise inversely with iteration progress during exploitation, starting with 10\% parameter range noise and decreasing linearly to 1\%. This approach maintains local search capability while preventing excessive randomness that could undermine convergence. The strategy automatically adapts to parameter bounds, ensuring consistent relative perturbation magnitudes across different weight dimensions.

\subsection{Prediction Model}

The prediction model integrates optimized weights with sigmoid base risk calculation and comprehensive performance evaluation to generate probabilistic departure predictions through implementation of the Multi-Layer Probability Fusion Algorithm.

\subsubsection{Multi-Layer Probability Fusion Algorithm}

The core prediction algorithm implementation combines multiple information sources through a sophisticated fusion mechanism that performs base risk calculation using sigmoid function incorporating age and contract status, computes performance scores through weighted combination of percentile-based metrics, generates final probability through balanced fusion of base risk and performance factors, and applies boundary constraints by clamping output to [0.05, 0.95] range.

\subsubsection{Sigmoid Base Risk Implementation}

The sigmoid base risk function implementation utilizes the mathematical formulation defined in the design phase, integrating normalized age and contract factors through optimized alpha and tau parameters to capture domain-specific knowledge about player departure patterns.

\subsubsection{Composite Objective Function Implementation}

The optimization process implementation maximizes the composite score formulation specified in the design phase, prioritizing ranking quality through PR-AUC weighting whilst maintaining balanced classification performance and probability calibration through the integrated evaluation framework.
